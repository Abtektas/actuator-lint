# CA012: the thrust of a rotor points down or backwards

Level: error

## What it checks

The effectiveness matrix says that a rotor pushes the vehicle down, or
backwards. PX4's body axes point forwards, to the right and down, so a rotor
that lifts the vehicle has a negative thrust_z effectiveness and a rotor that
pushes it forwards a positive thrust_x effectiveness.

The direction of a rotor is the thrust axis it acts on most. The rule reports

- a rotor that acts mostly on thrust_z with a positive value: down
- a rotor that acts mostly on thrust_x with a negative value: backwards

It is applied to the vehicle types that [CA005](CA005.md) knows:
multirotor, fixed-wing, standard VTOL, tiltrotor VTOL, tailsitter VTOL and
multirotor with tilt. For a multirotor (`CA_AIRFRAME 0`) only the downward
direction is looked at, and only when every rotor acts mostly on thrust_z. A
multirotor with rotors along other axes is an omnidirectional vehicle, such as
PX4's omnicopter airframes, whose rotors may point anywhere.

## Why it matters

PX4 computes the matrices from what the parameters say, not from how the
vehicle is built. When the vehicle is built correctly and a parameter has the
wrong sign, the mix gets the wrong sign too: in the examples below the
thrust_z mix entry of the rotor is +1 where the other rotors have -1, and the
thrust_x entry of the forward rotor is -1. A thrust setpoint then lowers the
command of that motor where it should raise it. PX4 reports none of this,
because the matrix is a valid one for the vehicle the parameters describe.

## Example

`examples/quad_rotor_down.params`: a quadrotor with `CA_ROTOR1_AZ 1`. The
default of the rotor axis is `CA_ROTORn_AZ -1`, which is up.

`examples/standard_vtol_pusher_backwards.params`: a standard VTOL whose
forward rotor has `CA_ROTOR4_AX -1`.

## What to do

Check the signs of the rotor named in the finding:

- the rotor axis `CA_ROTORn_AX`, `_AY`, `_AZ`. It is the direction of the
  thrust in the body frame: `AZ -1` is up, `AX 1` is forwards. PX4 does not
  read it for a fixed-wing, whose rotors always point forwards, and for a
  multirotor with tilt, whose rotors always point up.
- the thrust coefficient `CA_ROTORn_CT`. A negative value turns the thrust
  around.

A pusher propeller behind the vehicle and a puller in front of it both have
`AX 1`: the axis is the direction of the thrust, not the place of the rotor.

The rule looks at the sign of the main direction only. It does not find a
rotor axis that is tilted the wrong way, or a rotor at the wrong position.
