# CA006: an axis cannot be controlled independently of the others

Level: error; warning for an axis the vehicle type does not need

## What it checks

The effectiveness row of an axis is, or nearly is, a combination of the rows of
axes with a higher priority. The priority order is thrust_z, roll, pitch,
thrust_x, thrust_y, yaw. The finding names the rows the axis depends on.

The test is the one PX4 `main` applies to every matrix
(`ControlAllocationPseudoInverse::dropDependentAxes`, added after release/1.18
was branched): PX4 `main` removes such an axis from the matrix and reports it,
which is [CA002](CA002.md). PX4 v1.17.0 and release/1.18 do not have this
test, so the tool applies it itself when the engine does not report dropped
axes. With an engine that reports them this rule does not run.

The level follows the vehicle type, as for CA002: an error for an axis that
[CA005](CA005.md) lists as needed, and for vehicle types the tool knows nothing
about; a warning for an axis the vehicle type does not need, such as thrust_x
and thrust_y of a tailsitter with canted rotors.

## Why it matters

If the yaw row is a multiple of the thrust_z row, no combination of actuator
commands changes yaw without changing thrust by the same proportion. The
vehicle has one control less than the matrix seems to offer. PX4 versions
without the test compute a mix for the axis all the same, and the report shows
it as controlled.

## Example

`examples/quad_same_spin.params`: a quadrotor whose four rotors all have a
positive `CA_ROTORn_KM`, so they all turn the same way. The yaw row is a
multiple of the thrust_z row, and the mix commands all four motors alike for
yaw and for thrust.

`examples/hexa_rotor_count_4.params`: with two of six rotors not counted
([CA021](CA021.md)), yaw depends on roll and pitch.

## What to do

For an axis the vehicle needs, look at the rows the finding names:

- yaw of a multirotor that depends on thrust_z: the signs of `CA_ROTORn_KM`
  do not alternate
- an axis that depends on roll or pitch: rotors that all lie on one line, or
  control surfaces whose torque values are multiples of each other
- too few actuators: a matrix cannot control more axes than it has actuators

A warning for an axis the vehicle does not need can be left as it is. It says
that the actuators produce a force on that axis as a side effect of another
axis, which canted rotors do on purpose.

## How it was checked

The test was compared with PX4 itself: for 4400 random configurations of all
supported vehicle types, the axes the tool finds on the matrices of PX4
v1.17.0 are the axes PX4 `main` (8c243a2cd6) drops for the same parameters, in
all 6130 matrices; 3873 of them have at least one such axis.
