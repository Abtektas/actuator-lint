# CA005: an axis the vehicle cannot be flown without is not controlled

Level: error

## What it checks

No matrix of the configuration controls an axis that a vehicle of this type
needs:

| `CA_AIRFRAME` | Needed axes |
|---|---|
| 0 (multirotor), 8 (multirotor with tilt) | roll, pitch, yaw, thrust_z |
| 1 (fixed-wing) | roll, pitch |
| 2 (standard VTOL) | roll, pitch, yaw, thrust_z, thrust_x |
| 3 (tiltrotor VTOL), 4 (tailsitter VTOL) | roll, pitch, yaw, thrust_z |

An axis is controlled when at least one actuator has an effect on it in one of
the matrices and PX4 neither set the row to zero ([CA004](CA004.md)) nor
dropped it ([CA002](CA002.md)). The finding names which of these is the case.

Yaw is not needed for a fixed-wing, because a flying wing has none, and thrust
is not, because a glider has none. For a tiltrotor the matrices are the ones of
the hover position, so forward thrust is not looked at.

## Why it matters

PX4 computes a matrix for such a configuration without any error, and the
other rules have nothing to object to in it. But a setpoint on that axis
reaches no actuator: a multirotor without yaw, a plane without pitch, or a
standard VTOL without forward thrust does not do what the controller asks for.

## What to do

Find out why the axis has no effect. Frequent causes:

- roll or pitch of a multirotor: the rotor positions `CA_ROTORn_PX` and
  `CA_ROTORn_PY` are missing, so all rotors sit at the centre of gravity
- yaw of a multirotor: every moment coefficient `CA_ROTORn_KM` is zero
- thrust: every thrust coefficient `CA_ROTORn_CT` is zero, or the rotor axis
  `CA_ROTORn_AX`, `_AY`, `_AZ` is zero
- forward thrust of a standard VTOL: the forward rotor is not counted by
  `CA_ROTOR_COUNT` ([CA021](CA021.md)), or its axis is not `CA_ROTORn_AX 1`
- roll or pitch of a plane: the control surfaces have no torque
  (`CA_SV_CSn_TRQ_R`, `_TRQ_P`) or `CA_SV_CS_COUNT` is too small

The rule looks at whether an axis is controlled at all, not at whether it is
controlled well or in the right direction. A configuration without this
finding can still have rotors in the wrong place or with the wrong sign; a
rotor whose thrust points down or backwards is found by [CA012](CA012.md).
